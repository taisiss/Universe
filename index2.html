COMPOSITION

What is the universe made of?

composition

Everything we see (stars, planets, ourselves) is only 5% of the Universe. The remaining 95% is something we know almost nothing about.

68% — dark energy

27% — dark matter

5% — ordinary matter

DARK ENERFY

Dark energy is a hypothetical form of energy that uniformly fills all of space in the Universe and acts as "antigravity," causing it to expand at an accelerating rate. It accounts for roughly 68–70% of the total energy-mass of the Universe.

HOW IT WAS DISCOVERY:

In 1998, two independent teams of astronomers, studying distant Type Ia supernovae (which serve as "standard candles" for measuring cosmic distances), came to a shocking conclusion: the expansion of the Universe is not slowing down under the influence of gravity, as previously thought, but is accelerating. For this discovery, Saul Perlmutter, Brian Schmidt, and Adam Riess were awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2011.

PROBLEMS

Attempting to link dark energy to the quantum energy of vacuum leads to the "cosmological constant problem": theoretical calculations predict the vacuum energy to be 120 orders of magnitude (10^120 times) greater than what is observed. This colossal discrepancy is one of the greatest conflicts between theory and experiment in the history of physics

DARK MATTER

Dark matter is a form of matter that does not emit, absorb, or reflect light, but manifests itself through gravity. It makes up about 27% of the total energy-mass of the Universe and roughly 85% of all matter in it.

HOW IT WAS DISCOVERY:

In 1933, astronomer Fritz Zwicky, studying the motion of galaxies in the Coma Cluster, noticed that they were moving too fast. There was not enough visible mass to hold them together by gravity. He suggested the presence of an invisible substance and called it "dunkle Materie" (dark matter).Later, in the 1970s, Vera Rubin discovered the same problem while studying the rotation speeds of galaxies: outer stars rotate just as fast as inner ones, although they should slow down. This became the main proof of the existence of dark matter.

PROBLEMS

Despite decades of searching, dark matter has still not been detected directly. All evidence is indirect — through its gravitational influence on visible matter and light. This is one of the greatest mysteries of modern physics.

How it differs from dark energy:

• Dark matter — attracts and slows down expansion. It clumps together into galaxies and clusters.

• Dark energy — repels and accelerates expansion. It is uniformly distributed throughout space.
