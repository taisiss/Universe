ABOUT PROXIMA CENTAURI B

Proxima Centauri b

Proxima Centauri b is the closest exoplanet to us, located just 4.2 light-years from Earth. It orbits the red dwarf star Proxima Centauri and is considered one of the main candidates for potential habitability. In its characteristics, the planet is similar to Earth: its mass is about 1.27 times that of Earth, and according to models, it is rocky. It is located in the "habitable zone," where liquid water could exist, and a year on it lasts only 11.2 days. Scientists consider it potentially habitable because NASA's climate models show that even with tidal locking (when one side always faces the star), ocean currents are capable of transferring heat and sustaining liquid water. In addition, high salinity could lower the freezing point.

PROBLEMS

However, there are also serious problems. Proxima Centauri is a "flare-prone" star, and the planet receives 250 times more X-ray radiation than Earth, which could strip away its atmosphere. Moreover, due to tidal locking, one hemisphere is always illuminated, while the other is plunged into eternal night.

ABOUT KEPLER-442 B

Kepler-442 b

A Kepler-442 b is a confirmed Earth-sized exoplanet and one of the most promising candidates for habitability. It is located about 1,100–1,200 light-years from Earth in the constellation Lyra. In its characteristics, it is similar to Earth: its radius is 1.34 times that of Earth, and its mass is about 2.3 times greater. It has a 60% probability of being rocky. It is located in the "habitable zone," and a year on it lasts about 112 days. Scientists consider it potentially habitable because it receives about two-thirds of the light that Earth receives from the Sun. The probability of it being in the habitable zone is 97%. Its star is a quiet orange dwarf that can live up to 30 billion years, which provides time for the development of life.

PROBLEMS

However, there are problems: surface gravity is about 30% stronger than on Earth, and a 2021 study showed that oxygenic photosynthesis may be impossible there. Due to the enormous distance, direct observations of its atmosphere are not yet possible.

ABOUT LHS 1140 B

TLHS 1140 b is a confirmed rocky exoplanet in the habitable zone and one of the best candidates in the search for life. It is located 48 light-years from Earth in the constellation Cetus. In its characteristics, it is similar to Earth: its radius is 1.73 times that of Earth, and its mass is about 5.6 times greater. It is located in the "habitable zone," and a year on it lasts about 24.7 days. Its main distinction is the first confirmed atmosphere on a rocky planet in the habitable zone. In 2026, helium escaping from its atmosphere was detected, proving that its gaseous envelope has been preserved for more than 3 billion years. This is possible because its star is a very quiet red dwarf without powerful flares.

PROBLEMS

However, there are uncertainties: surface gravity is 70% stronger than on Earth, the exact composition of the atmosphere is unknown, and the average temperature without the greenhouse effect is about −47°C.
