ABOUT BLACK HOLE

black hole This is a region in space with such powerful gravity that even light cannot escape it. It forms when a massive star runs out of fuel and collapses under its own weight. At the center is a singularity — a point of infinite density. Around it is the event horizon, a boundary beyond which there is no escape. Anything that crosses this boundary (gas, dust, light) is pulled inside and disappears forever. The greater the mass of the black hole, the wider its event horizon.

Black holes are not "vacuum cleaners" — they don't suck up everything. If the Sun turned into a black hole, Earth would continue to orbit it along the same path, it would just become dark and cold.

The first photo of a black hole was presented on April 10, 2019. It was made by the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) project with the support of NASA. Scientists photographed a supermassive black hole at the center of the galaxy M87, located 55 million light-years from Earth.

ABOUT QUASAR

QUASAR

A quasar is the glowing matter around a black hole, the bright active core of a distant galaxy that looks like a star but shines thousands of times brighter than the entire Milky Way.

INTERESTING FACT

Even though quasars look like tiny stars, they are actually enormous. The accretion disk around their black hole can be the size of several Solar Systems. And the black hole at the center can weigh as much as billions of Suns.

At its center is a supermassive black hole. It pulls in gas and dust, which heat up to millions of degrees and glow. Why is it important? These are some of the brightest objects in the Universe. Their light travels to us for billions of years, helping scientists study the early history of space.

ABOUT NEUTRON STAR

NEUTRON STAR

A neutron star is a superdense core left after the explosion of a massive star. It is so dense that a teaspoon of its matter would weigh billions of tons — like Mount Everest.
